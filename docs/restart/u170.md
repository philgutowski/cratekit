**U170 built, 2026-11-01, in the cloud lane on
`cloud/351-crate-status-stops-listing-weeks`, issue #351: `crate status` stops
listing weeks old open gates one by one, and one command retires them.**
One predicate in `src/cratekit/cli/gates.py`, `old_gate`, decides whether an
open gate is old: idle for longer than the threshold, measured from the later
of the run's `created` stamp and its record's last write, so a run resumed
yesterday is new however long ago it started, and a record whose time cannot be
read is new. An old gate is retirable when its verb still declares it and its
options carry `abort`; otherwise it is kept and the reason said. `crate status`
folds every retirable gate into one line, `runs.old`: the count, the dates they
span and `crate run retire --older-than Nd`. Newer gates, kept gates (the two
`sync.pair` gates offer `apply` and `skip`, no `abort`) and unreadable records
are listed one by one as before. `open_runs` still counts every open run, and
the summary gains `old_gates`.

`crate run retire [--older-than Nd]`, registry entry `run.retire`, plans every
retirable gate except its own run's, asks once at the ordinary gate
`run.retire.confirm` (apply or abort, the digest over each planned run, verb,
gate and idle date), and on `apply` records `abort` as actor `session` on each,
explicit gates included, never the go option. It lists what it retired, what it
kept and why, and any record it could not read. Nothing old asks nothing and
ends with no change. A gate answered between the plan and the write is said as
`moved` and left as it is. `--older-than` takes whole days, 1 to 3650, written
`7d`; anything else is the argument parser's usage refusal. The threshold is the
new `reports.open_gate_stale_days`, seven by default, validated like the other
day counts with a ceiling of ten years. `docs/GUIDE.md` says so in the status
section, the gate table and a new "Retiring old gates" section.

Two calls made without Phillip, both conservative. A gate whose options have no
`abort` is never retired, since `skip` on `sync.pair` is a go answer for the
other source and not a no. And the age runs from the later of the two clocks,
so a record restored from a copy reads as new rather than old. No producer of
`RunState.pending` exists in `src` (the pending actions are the session
record's), so a run holding both an open gate and a pending action has no
branch of its own.
