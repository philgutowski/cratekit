**What landed.** The seam is Discogs', not `titles.matches`, which every
source reads and which is unchanged, so Beatport, Traxsource and Bandcamp
read their rows as before (the brief forbids a Beatport change; R-1637 is
its mirror). `tagging/titles.py` gains two readers built from the parse's
own pieces, no second parser:
- `named_versions(FileName)`: the mixes a titled file's title names by
  their maker, each bracket group the title part ends with and the dash
  part right after the title that end in a version word (or "Rmx"),
  known by their words with the domain's `VERSION_WORDS`, a remaster's
  year and the new `MIX_DESCRIPTIONS` left out at either end and folded as
  a name is (accents, "and", "the", a possessive "s"). "Tim Green Remix"
  names `tim green`; "Extended Mix", "Original Mix", "Vocal Mix", "LP
  Version" and "2009 Digital Remaster" name no one, so such a file matches
  as it always did. A title of brackets alone, one after a lone track
  number or the marker, an empty title part, a credit group "(feat. ...)",
  an ambiguous name and an unbracketed version name nothing.
- `writes_version(row, named, titled=...)`: whether a catalogue title writes
  one of them. For a row whose title is the file's: a group ending the row
  or one of its dash parts, any dash part after the first, the row whole,
  or an unbracketed whole ending in the maker. For a single's other track,
  only a row that is the mix alone ("Tim Green Remix", "(Tim Green Remix)").

`searchers._release_carries` replaces `_release_matches`: a track matching
title and artist counts only when it writes the file's named mix, and the
single fallback only when the release title writes it or a track is the mix
alone. A release refused only for its mix turns the miss's note into
"Discogs release N holds no track with this version (tim green)", carried
into the file's record under `answers`; the walk goes on to Beatport as
after any miss. A file naming no mix is matched exactly as on `main`.
