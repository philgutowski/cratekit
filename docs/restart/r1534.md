**R-1534, #713, a title that strips to nothing matches no other track, in
every source, built in the cloud lane on 2026-11-12.** Filed at the review of
#673 (U244). Large: the matcher decides which catalogue row's genre is
written onto a file, for good.

**What failed, reproduced on the base.** The shared `_title_matches` in
`tagging/searchers.py` stripped a trailing bracket from the first "(" to the
last ")", so "(Intro)" or "(Not Just) Knee Deep (Part 1)" stripped to the
empty string, which is in every title: any row by the artist matched.
Beatport's `_beatport_best` stripped the same way on its own, and a title of
no letter or digit, or a Beatport credit with no name, read as contained in
everything too. Driven on the base through `tag.tag_one`: a FLAC titled
"(Intro)" took Deep House off another track by the artist. The tests written
first, `tests/test_title_matcher.py`, were 106 of 191 red on the base.

**What landed.** One reader of a title's core, `_title_core`, shared by every
source. The shared strip where it leaves a letter or digit; else, for a title
opening with bracket groups, the groups and the text after them, stripped the
same way ("(Not Just) Knee Deep"); else, for a title of brackets alone, the
words of its first groups, compared exactly and never by containment
("intro"). `_title_matches` refuses a side with no letter or digit
(`_identifies`), compares the cores with their brackets taken out, lets the
cores contain one another, and lets one whole title, suffix and all, contain
the other only when the contained side names more than a version
(`_contains`, `_VERSION_WORDS`). `_beatport_best` reads the same core: a
noise title scores nothing, a row whose name has no letter or digit is passed
over, a credit with no name is dropped, a title of brackets alone scores only
a row it equals, and its middle tier reads the whole row name in the title's
core as `main` did, the title's core in the name's core, and, reaching into
the name's suffix, only a title naming more than a version. Discogs moved
onto the shared matcher: `_discogs_title_matches` and its copy of the last
group strip are gone, and `tests/test_discogs_searcher.py` calls
`_title_matches`. Traxsource and Bandcamp read it as before.

**Kept, and measured.** An unfiltered differential of `main`'s matcher
against this one over 319 titles (the suite's own fixture titles and the
review's) loses, where both sides carry letters and `main`'s strip left
something, only pairs in which one side is version words alone read inside
the other's bracketed suffix ("Mix" against "Sunrise Mood (Original Mix)").
Beatport's scorer over the same titles and five mixes loses only rows named by
version words alone. What it gains is the same title written with and without
its brackets.

**Decided here, recorded.** The version word rule (plan point 3), narrowed by
round one to a containment that reaches into a bracketed suffix, since
between two cores it lost "Original" its own "Original feat. X". A title of
version words alone still matches an identical row. Code conventions (the
core reader, the test module, its tables) were the session's to decide.
