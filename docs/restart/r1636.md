**R-1636, #781, a file's title is read from where it starts, never from a
dash part that is not its title, built in the cloud lane on 2026-11-12.**
Filed at the review of #770 (R-1631). Large: the matcher decides which
catalogue row's genre is written onto a file, for good.

**What failed, reproduced on the base.** Since R-1631 the file's side of the
record shop matcher in `tagging/searchers.py` (`_readings`, `searched=True`)
was read from any run of its dash parts, because the live library writes
`KEY - BPM - ` into the title tag and an untagged file is asked by its name.
So a version tail with a word `VERSION_WORDS` lacks ("08A - 124 - Other Song
- Vocal Mix" as "vocal mix"), an untagged name's album ("08A - 124 - Tide -
Artist - Sun Path" as "sun path") and its artist ("08A - 124 - Artist - Sun
Path" as the artist) were each read as the title, and another recording by
the artist wrote its genre. The tests written first: through `tag_one` on the
base, 12 of the 13 title tag shapes and all 3 untagged name shapes wrote
another recording's genre.

**What landed.** The searched artist reaches the matcher: `_title_matches`
takes it as a required keyword, `_beatport_best` reads the title with it, and
Traxsource, Bandcamp and Discogs pass it; the `Searcher` protocol is
unchanged, since the searcher already holds the artist. A file's title is read
from one place, `_title_starts`:
- after its head, bounded to what the naming verb writes (`_head_end`): a
  Camelot key, a tempo, or one of each, a number after a key being its tempo
  whatever its value and a tempo alone only in a tempo's range, so "909" of
  "08A - 124 - 909 - Artist - Album" is the title; and after a track number
  written alone, "01" to "09", never before a version;
- at the first part that does not credit the artist (`_names_the_artist`):
  one of the names it joins on "&", ",", "/", ";", "+", "x", "vs", "pres" or
  a feature word is one of the artist's names (`_artist_names`), accents
  folded and "The" aside, past a "[MP3]" marker and a glued track number on
  the first part; or the part, two words or more, a run of a name with
  initials ("B. Badie"). A title holding the artist's name is a title
  ("Riding The Tide", "Bernard Badie Theme"), and so is one word of it;
- no later part is read alone, save a track numbered title ("02 Title", or
  "02 - Title"), and the part before one is never read alone either; a run
  from the title may end before a version, a head, or a part crediting the
  artist ("Title - Artist - Album"), and anywhere when the title follows the
  head directly;
- the artist's part is the title only when nothing else can be: "Artist -
  Original Mix", whose other part is version words alone, and "Artist -
  Artist - Album", whose next part credits the artist too, the second then
  read as well;
- and "Artist - X" with X itself ending in a version is read both ways,
  since only a list could tell a title X from a self titled track and its
  version (round one's B1, recorded below).
A track number is shed only from a run whose first part opens with one, so
"007 - Original Mix" is no longer read as its version, as it was on `main`.
The catalogue side is unchanged.

**Kept, and measured.** Three unfiltered differentials of `main`'s matcher
against the branch, over the string constants of eleven tagging test files
(1,166 to 1,327 titles as the suite grew), every gain and every loss read by
hand:
- Plain titles, and each wrapped in seven name shapes with the exact artist
  (head, artist first, title then artist, title artist album, the track
  numbered album form): for a title of one part no correct match is lost in
  any shape, and every new match is the title itself ("Title - Artist" with
  no head and a number after a full head, both misses on `main`). Every
  plain loss is a card shape, a wrong match `main` made (the tempo "124" off
  "08A - 124 - Interlude", "Kerri Chandler Remix" off "Sunrise Mood - Kerri
  Chandler Remix"), or a catalogue row carrying a head or an artist prefix,
  which no catalogue the matcher reads writes.
- The artist written 14 ways in 11 name shapes (177,716 names): what is still
  lost is one word of the name, a collaboration on "and" or "with", and a
  track number glued to the artist mid name, recorded below.
- The artist's name inside the title, in eight shapes: no correct match lost;
  the only new matches are "Artist - Artist & Tide" and corpus strings no name
  writes.
Reading the build's own draft this way found three defects before review: a
title ending in a version word fell back to the artist, an unbounded head ate
"909", and an album was read before a track numbered title that ended in a
version word. And the round one fix's differential, wrapping titles only in
the exact artist string, missed the round two class: a measurement on a
fixture, now in the solutions entry.

**Decided here, recorded.** The album is not handed in: the searcher is never
given it, and the album of an untagged name is told by its position after the
artist's part. A file with no artist tag reads its name's first part only; it
is unreachable on the default walk, which refuses a file with no artist. The
both ways reading of "Artist - X" keeps `main`'s behaviour where only a list
could decide (a residual with an issue). "and" and "with" join no names in a
title. Bracketed alternate titles stay refused (R-1638's recommended option,
open on #770). Code conventions, the helpers, the tests and the tables were
the session's to decide.
