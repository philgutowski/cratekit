**U280, #791, the title matcher reads a name once into typed parts, with
ambiguity an outcome, built in the cloud lane on 2026-11-13.** Filed by the
R-1646 thread after four fixes in one day (R-1534, R-1631, R-1636, R-1646),
each review finding the defect the fix before it made. Large: the matcher
decides which catalogue row's genre is written onto a file, for good.

**What landed.** The record shop matcher is `tagging/titles.py`, and
`searchers.py` keeps `_title_matches(searched, found, *, artist)` and
Beatport's three tiers, so Traxsource, Bandcamp and Discogs' release reader
change nowhere; the old readers (`_readings`, `_meet`, `_title_starts` and
their helpers) are gone.
- `Title`: one stretch of text read as a title, known by its words and its
  core, each with and without its feature credit and version run; two meet
  when a name of one is a name of the other (`Title.same`).
- `Row`: a catalogue row read by its own structure, its title before
  trailing brackets or a dash version tail, and the splits of title from
  version its brackets, dash tail or Beatport's mix write.
- `FileName`: a file's title or name read once into its parts, each with a
  `Role` (head, track number, the artist's credit, title, album, after the
  title) and how it was told (`Told`: shape, position, credit, list), and an
  `Outcome`: unreadable, titled, or ambiguous where only a list of version
  words could tell, which matches only a row writing the same split or the
  whole name.
- `matches(FileName, Row)`, one function.

What changed from `main`, each deliberately and measured: R-1651's first two
shapes are ambiguous (a feature credit after the version, more than one of
the artist's own credits before it); the naming verb's `[MP3]` marker is
read past where it writes one, before a title of brackets and a lone track
number; the whole name is read as written, never shed across its dashes, and
a bracket group is shed only from the part carrying it (`own_core`, in the
shared match and Beatport's middle tier alike); a track numbered later title
of brackets alone, "02 (Intro)", is a title; and what the old cross dash
shedding found rightly is read by name instead (`_featured_first_part`, with
no head before it as `main` read it; `_tempo_as_title`).

200,000 Beatport pages, half of them the name's own title. The first commit
re-expressed `main` exactly: no difference over 394,160 names and the
Beatport sample. Every later change was made alone and every difference it
made classified against the commit before it; against `main` at the head,

- R-1651's shapes, now ambiguous: 2,280 lost (rows reading them one way) and
  194 gained (rows writing their split);

  Original Mix" taking "Original Mix", "Artist - (Intro)" the self titled

Beatport's scorer: 72 gained and 168 lost of 200,000 pages, in the same
