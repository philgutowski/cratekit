**R-1308, #558, a removal from an account playlist whose read lags the
removal is taken as done, built in the cloud lane on 2026-11-06.** Found at
round one of U220's review (#543): `remove_tracks` read each removal back once
and refused at once unless the reading was the playlist with exactly those
positions gone, so on a provider whose read lags a write it accepted, the
lag U220 measured for a move on Sitting 62, a removal that landed was refused
either as "nothing was taken off it" (the declared count current, the tracks
lagging) or as "a track other than the asked ones may have come off" (both
lagging).

**What landed.** `TidalAccount.remove_tracks` reads each removal back through
`_removal_read_back`, on the order's `ORDER_REREADS` and `ORDER_PAUSES`. Each
reading is judged on its two halves, the tracks read and the declared count,
and each must be the playlist before the removal or after it, with no video
declared; a removal always takes a track off, so the two counts differ and a
half is never both. Both after is done, and only that reading is remembered
as the playlist's membership and carried as the next request's positions and
tag. Either half still before, the other before or after, is a lag: read again
after a pause, at most three times, then refused `outbound-remove-refused`,
problem `read-back-differs`, `lagged: yes`, naming which half lagged and saying
the removal may have landed with nothing more sent. Anything else, a wrong
track gone, a count that is neither, a video, refuses at once with no pause,
`lagged: no`, with the old wording. Every refusal forgets what the adapter
remembered of the playlist, and the step names no one verb, since four callers
take a removal. The removal is never sent again. `_reading` was split out of
`_positions`, whose refusal and callers are unchanged. The U220 provider stand
in in `tests/test_outbound_order.py` gained a removal by position with the
same tag and lag and a lag of the count alone; the tests are
`tests/test_outbound_remove_lag.py`, written failing first (21 of them on the
base). `docs/GUIDE.md` says what a DJ sees.

**Decided conservatively, recorded here.** A removal answered 412 changed
nothing but is still raised as a failure rather than read again, as the brief
kept it out of scope (a residual). A read back whose count or videos cannot be
read, which main refused at source as nothing taken off, now stops as a
possibly wrong track (a residual, the more conservative answer). The residual
files are named `r1308-...` for the card's own residual, where the README's
form names a unit; `board.py residuals` reads them all.
