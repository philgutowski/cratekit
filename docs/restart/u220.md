**What landed.** The account adapter's `order_tracks` moves every track to the
FRONT, the one move whose landing does not depend on how the provider counts a
move's target: the longest run at the tail of the asked order already standing
in order is kept, and the asked tracks before it are moved to the front, the
last of them first. Its first reading must agree with what is known: the
caller's `expect` (exactly, in order) or what this command's own adds and reads
left (as a multiset); a reading that does not is read again after pauses of 1,
2 and 4 seconds, at most three times, then refused with nothing moved. A move
answered 412 changed nothing (the client sends the tag the reading came with),
so the playlist is read again, held to the same agreement, and the moves
planned afresh, at most three times; the 412 is found on either the cause or
the context chain, since the client raises a decode error from a body that is
not JSON. No other move failure is ever sent again. A read back still equal to
the playlist before the move is a lag, read again on the same pauses; anything
else is refused at once. From any one reading at most one move per asked track
is made, since each grows the kept tail by one, and more is refused as
`no-progress` (added after the mutation table's first run hung on a planner
that never finished). In `sync pair`, an order refusal of
`outbound-remove-refused` or `unavailable-transient` stops its pair alone: the
step `order-refused` is recorded, the pair stays unfinished and its snapshot
does not move, every other pair and the rekordbox side are written, and after
the loop the command stops once, naming each unordered playlist, the finished
count and `crate --run RUN sync pair`, carrying every refusal's steps once. A
continue hands the order the check's own fresh listing as `expect`. Any other
refusal, a rejected session among them, still stops the whole write.
`pair_calls._moves` counts moves by the same front rule. `docs/GUIDE.md` says
what a DJ sees.
