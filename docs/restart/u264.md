**U264, #693, `crate analyze run` mixes a stereo track to mono with one add,
the same bits as librosa's mean, built in the cloud lane on 2026-11-12.**
Filed from the speed survey of 2026-11-12: `librosa.load(..., mono=True)`
mixes with `np.mean` over a transposed view, about a quarter of a second of
every decode of a long stereo track, paid at 22,050 Hz by the head and again
at 32,000 Hz by the vocal pass. Medium: it changes how the analysis decodes
and writes nothing, though its samples feed the tag write.

**What the card's plain add would have changed.** Measured before any code,
on librosa 0.10.2, 0.10.2.post1, 0.11.0 and 1.0.0 on numpy 2.5.4, and
0.10.2.post1 on numpy 1.26.4: `(left + right) / 2` is not bit identical to
`librosa.to_mono`. It differs only in a zero's sign. numpy's add reduction
starts from its identity, plus zero, so on every release two minus zero
channels mean to plus zero; and librosa 1.0 rewrote `to_mono` to add the
mean into a buffer of zeros, so on 1.0 a half sum that rounds to minus zero
(minus the smallest subnormal and zero) is plus zero as well. The card's
snippet compared with `array_equal`, which reads minus zero as equal to plus
zero, so it would have passed. A minus zero reaches the mix from a float WAV,
an MP3 through libsndfile, or ffmpeg's float decode.

**The mix, `decode.mono`,** in the one decoder U263 built for it:

1. `decode.load` reads through `librosa.load(path, sr=None, mono=False)`
   with the same window, the same read as before and, on an older release,
   the same audioread fallback, then mixes with `mono` and resamples with
   `librosa.resample(res_type="soxr_hq")`, the two calls `librosa.load`
   makes after its read. All three stay inside the same `try`, so a refusal
   is caught where it was. The ffmpeg path mixes through the same helper.
   The card's approach read with `soundfile` directly; reading through
   librosa is the same read and keeps the older releases' fallback without
   restating it, a convention call recorded here.
2. Two float32 channels on a release named in `_MEASURED_MIX` (exactly
   0.10.2, 0.10.2.post1, 0.11.0, 1.0.0) take `left + right`, `+= 0`,
   `/= 2`, and on 1.0 a second `+= 0`, after librosa's own `valid_audio`,
   so a sample that is not finite is refused with the same error. A finite
   pair whose float32 sum overflows overflows as librosa's reduction does
   and is refused by the resampler as before.
3. Anything else, mono, three or more channels, another dtype, a patch or
   pre release or any release not measured, is `librosa.to_mono` itself:
   a future release can lose the speed, never change a sample.

**Proved,** by the bits and never `array_equal`, on all five installs:
`tests/test_analysis_decode.py` (the half sum against a float64 expectation
the code does not compute, the zero sign table read from each release, the
release table; numpy only, so it runs on the slim install and in CI) and
`tests/test_analysis_decode_librosa.py` (the installed release's own

and 32,000 Hz over generated stereo float and 16 bit WAV, FLAC and MP3 with
silence in both signs, minus zero frames, subnormals, a clipped run and an
odd length, a mono and a three channel file, over an offset and a duration;
the ffmpeg path on minus zero frames; NaN, both infinities and two
overflowing pairs refused with librosa's message, `skipped-undecodable` from
the head and no vocal samples; the head of WAV, FLAC and MP3 equal to the
head with librosa's own `load`). U263's test that an older librosa's M4A is
"returned untouched" now asserts its read, mixed and resampled, is the bits
its own `load` gives, and a librosa stand in in `test_analysis_workers.py`
carries `to_mono` and `resample`. Measured on a generated 345 second stereo
FLAC, median of five, bits equal: 812 to 501 ms at 22,050 Hz and 845 to 615
ms at 32,000 Hz on 1.0.0, 707 to 523 and 937 to 773 ms on 0.10.2.post1.
