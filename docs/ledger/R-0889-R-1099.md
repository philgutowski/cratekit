## R-889. A track a prune took off a label year playlist is never put back, even once its hold is lifted

**Found:** by the built in review of R-836 on `relay/254`, 2026-10-03.

**The mechanism.** `label prune` moves a taken track from `added` to
`pruned`, and `label playlists` treats a pruned track as asked already, so
the build never adds it again. Without that the build, which has no genre
gate (R-872), would put back every held release's tracks the prune just took
off. Nothing clears `pruned`: a hold a person returns, a release the verifier
confirms again, or a label kind changed so the gate passes it, leaves the
track off its year playlist for good.

**Measured cost.** Driven in the suite only. A release a DJ returned from the
gate stays missing from its year playlist, with no verb to put it back.

**Closes when:** the author decides R-872. If the build follows the gate, it
can drop the pruned exclusion and judge holds itself; if not, a prune needs a
way to forget a track whose evidence is gone.

**Not on the board bar.** The conservative reading of a decision that is the
author's, tied to R-872.

## R-890. Each removal chunk opens and reads the whole playlist twice

**Found:** by the built in review of R-836 on `relay/254`, 2026-10-03.

**The mechanism.** `prune.apply` asks `remove_tracks` fifty tracks at a time,
so each chunk is recorded as it lands, and each call opens the playlist,
reads every position, removes, and reads every position again to check. The
read back of one chunk is not reused as the next chunk's read, since they are
separate calls. A prune of two hundred tracks off a thousand track playlist
is about eight full paged reads.

**Measured cost.** Provider calls, paced by the gate, on an account the
provider has rate limited before. Not measured live.

**Closes when:** the first live prune is timed at an attended sitting and the
author decides whether recording per chunk is worth the reads.

**Not on the board bar.** A cost in time.

## R-892. An exported playlist written before rows were kept is pruned by match alone

**Found:** by the built in review of R-836 on `relay/254`, 2026-10-03.

**The mechanism.** Since R-836 the export records the library row each track
was written from, and a prune takes a track off by its row. A track written
before has no row, so it leaves when no row of the library playlist matches it
now. That reading is guarded: the playlist is left alone when any row matched
nothing, or when any row matches a track the playlist was never written with.
A match that moved to ANOTHER track already recorded here, two rows swapping
their matches, passes both guards.

**Measured cost.** Not measured. Needs two rows of one crate whose provider
matches exchange between two exports.

**Closes when:** an `export playlist` run over each pre R-836 playlist has
recorded its rows, or the author accepts the guard as it stands.

**Not on the board bar.** Unmeasured reachability.

## R-894. A product made playlist holding a track the account cannot play cannot be pruned

**Found:** while building R-836 on `relay/254`, 2026-10-03.

**The mechanism.** The provider leaves a track the account cannot play out of
the listing and still counts it, so the read positions no longer match the
provider's. `remove_tracks` refuses `outbound-remove-refused` with nothing sent
rather than remove by a shifted position. The client's remove by identifier
reads one page and the same shifted positions, so it is no way round. A
playlist holding a video is refused the same way.

**Measured cost.** Not measured live. A year playlist one of whose tracks went
unavailable after the build cannot lose any track until that one is taken off
by hand.

**Closes when:** the first live prune says whether this occurs, and if it
does, a removal by a stable item identifier is found on the provider.

**Not on the board bar.** The refusal is honest and names the remedy.

## R-895. A large roster's every listed release can reach a verifier read that climbs no retry ladder

**Found:** by the built in review of R-888 on `relay/257`, 2026-10-03.

**The mechanism.** `label playlists` verifies every release a roster lists,
with no window, and `TidalCatalogue.search_release` reads a release again by
identifier when its listed copy carries no copyright line. That read is a bare
`session.album` call. The provider gate paces it, and nothing retries it, so a
rate limit on one of them ends the plan through the lookup failure path where
`_album` would have waited and asked again. Before R-888 a roster over 10,000
releases refused before any verification; now Mango Alley's 27,002 releases
all reach the verifier.

**Measured cost.** Not measured. How many of a large roster's listed releases
lack a copyright line, and so cost a read, is unmeasured, and so is whether
the paced rate draws a rate limit at that volume. The verb refuses and writes
nothing when it happens.

**Closes when:** the attended Mango Alley plan R-888 owes is timed and counts
its re-reads, and either it finishes or `search_release` reads through
`_album`'s ladder.

**Not on the board bar.** The reachability is unmeasured, and a refusal that
writes nothing is honest about what it could not read.

**Closed 2026-10-07, ledger triage.** Its closing condition allows "either it finishes": cutover sitting 30 and R-888's close record the Mango Alley plan reading the full roster, 334 confirmed and 11,864 not, in 3,052 provider calls, with no rate limit, and then applying; the wall time and a separate re-read count were not recorded, and `search_release` still uses a bare `session.album`.

## R-896. A batch name given with --name and --pass together lands into a folder already holding it

**Found:** by the built in review of U110 on `relay/258`, 2026-10-03.

**The mechanism.** Before U110 the intake file was named for the batch, so a
second landing under one `--name` was refused by the file the first had
written. The file is now named for the pass, so `--name A --pass P1` and later
`--name A --pass P2` write two files, and the second moves its tracks into the
folder `A` already is. The native writer then adds them to the `A` playlist,
and the xml splice replaces the export's `A` node with the second batch alone.
A name the landing chose is rechecked under the lock and never reused; a name a
person gave is theirs, and the native suite pins landing into one again.

**Measured cost.** Not measured. No printer prints both flags, and nothing
drove a person typing them.

**Closes when:** a person given name is refused over an existing folder on
both media, if the second landing into one name is not wanted.

**Not on the board bar.** The input is a hand typed combination no verb
produces, and the reachability is unmeasured.

## R-901. A batch past expiry that is still in INBOX moves to Old, and the next run removes it

## R-905. Tracks the library already owned are not added to an account playlist's INBOX playlist

**Found:** by U113 on `relay/261`, 2026-10-03, a decision the brief asked for.

**The mechanism.** A landing gives each playlist the author made on the
account its own INBOX playlist, holding the files THIS landing brought in for
it. A row the sweep resolved as owned was not downloaded and is not added, so
the INBOX playlist mirrors the account playlist only when the library held
none of its tracks. Chosen because INBOX is the listening queue `route inbox`
and `inbox rotate` work over, and an owned track is usually already filed
under BY GENRE, where it would be queued a second time. Mirroring would also
need each owned file's row in the collection, found by path, on both media,
which no verb does yet. The author's word was that the landing carries his
playlist into INBOX, which can be read either way.

**The options.** (a) As built, new tracks only. (b) Mirror: add every owned
row too, read from the intake report's `paths`, which needs a path to row
lookup in the export and in the database. (c) Mirror into a second playlist
outside INBOX, so the queue holds only what is new.

**Measured cost.** None measured; it is a choice of contents, and the files
and rows are all where they were.

**Closes when:** the author picks one at an attended sitting, reading the next
real sync of one of his playlists in rekordbox.

**Not on the board bar.** Nothing is lost or said falsely, and it closes only
by the author's choice.

**Closed by #295, U128, 2026-10-10, on `relay/295`.** The author chose (b) on
2026-10-10, mirror: his custom playlists sat in INBOX thin or empty because a
landing added only the files it downloaded. A landing now reads every report
of each account playlist's job in item order, which is the account playlist's
order. A carried row tied to a planned file is a new member, an owned row an
owned member carrying the report's `paths`, and every other row is left and
named in one warning, `account-playlist-left-out`. Each owned member's row is
found by path before the gate through one rule, `account_playlists.
resolve_owned`: the database's candidate rows on the native medium, the
Fresh Export's collection entries on the bridge, where the intake playlist
names the export's own TrackID; a file two active rows claim is not an
answer. The screen counts the owned tracks, the digest covers each one's row,
and the bridge engine reads the export again before its first move. Each
record entry names the account and the pass, and a later landing of the same
pass holds only its new files for a playlist that pass mirrored, so a rerun
adds nothing twice. The concern the decision weighed, an owned track queued
in INBOX a second time, is real and is filed as its own residual by U128.

file created that moment, no file has ever carried the value. The binding's
random id is uniform under 2 to the 28th, 268,435,456, and the bound on the
library's volume read 1,415,747 on 2026-10-09, so about one random id in
190 falls under the bound and is judged `WITHIN`, the class rekordbox
tolerates, and is left unrenumbered with no mention. On a volume whose
counter has passed the binding's range, the boot volume of this machine
among them at over 1.6 billion, every random id falls under the bound; the
plan then renumbers nothing and the gate and the envelope say so
(`rows-file-id-undecidable`). The same answer is given on a volume the mount
table does not name as APFS, since exFAT synthesizes inode numbers and HFS+
can wrap them, so a fresh file's inode bounds nothing there; the review of
U115 found both this and the volume check below.

**Measured cost.** On the live library, seventeen rows carry a random id
(the A8 row `73894078` and the sixteen sampler rows), so the expected miss
is under a tenth of a row; the live run's `renumbered` count against
seventeen says whether any was missed, and one left reads as stale and
plays no better than before. On a volume past the binding's range the
repair cannot help at all, and says so rather than guessing. No other
discriminator was found: the volume's files cannot be walked for the id,
since a stale id's inode has usually been freed and would read as absent
too.

**Closes when:** a row the landing made is told by a record rather than by
size, or the live run reports seventeen and the count is accepted as the
whole population. Ledger only: the rows at risk are few and named, every
row the landing makes from this unit on carries its inode, and no DJ
workflow is blocked by it beyond what R-928 already records.

## R-1091. A full label playlists build stopped by the provider's breaker keeps nothing it read, so the next run starts the crawl over

**Found:** at the build of #402, U191, 2026-10-31.

**The mechanism.** `crate label playlists LABEL` without `--releases` reads the
listing, then every listed release's tracks through
`catalogue.ReleaseTracks`, two provider calls a release, all in memory. A
breaker trip part way raises out of `_grouped` in
`src/cratekit/label/playlists_cli.py` and everything read is dropped, so the
next run lists and reads from the first release again. The same is true after
a clean read: the explicit gate exits, and the run that carries out the answer
reads the whole catalogue a second time. The brief's fourth requirement asked
for a resume or for the reason there is none.

**Why it is not built in U191.** A resumable read needs a store of release
reads that outlives the run, and a store like that has to decide how long a
read stays true: a release whose tracks the provider changed after it was
cached would be planned from the old rows, and the gate's digest would bind an
answer to a catalogue nobody read that day. That is a choice about what a
person approves, not about code, and it was not this unit's to make without
asking. U191 made the cost visible instead: over 500 estimated calls a full
build stops at `label.playlists.crawl` before any release is read, and a label
sync's releases reach the year playlists through `--releases` without the
crawl.

**Measured cost.** On 2026-10-31 a full build of Defected (12,368 releases
listed) spent 3,026 provider calls, met 43 rate limited answers and was parked
for 15 minutes before any plan was shown; the estimate now in front of that
gate is about 24,700 calls and nearly seven hours at the default pace, so a
full build of a label that size cannot finish inside one breaker window and
starts over each time. Not driven live in the cloud.

**Closes when:** a full build keeps its release reads per label in library
state, with a stated age past which a read is taken again, resumes from them
after a breaker trip or a gate, and shows on its screen how many reads were
reused and how old the oldest is. Earns an issue: a workflow a DJ needs, the
first full build of a large label, cannot finish (board bar c).
