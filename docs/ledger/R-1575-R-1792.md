## R-1575. The stealing suite's real runs pass under xdist's default distribution most of the time, so only the two pins catch work stealing being dropped

**Found:** at the review of #680, U251, 2026-11-12.

**The mechanism.** `tests/_stealing_suite.py` writes a suite in which, under
`--dist worksteal`, a queued test must move to the idle worker, and the
moved test's failure is what the driver and the battery are each asked to
report. Under `--dist load` a queued test usually moves too, because the
default distribution hands the holding worker only a short first chunk. So
the three tests that run the suite (`tests/test_suite_distribution.py`,
`tests/test_mutation_driver.py` and `tests/test_cloud_battery.py`) pass under
either distribution on most runs. The review drove it: under `--dist load`
the moved assertion failed in 4 of 22 runs, under `--dist loadgroup` in 5 of
12, and the holding test never timed out.

**Measured cost.** None while the two pins stand: dropping `--dist worksteal`
from `pyproject.toml`, or the driver stripping it, fails
`test_the_suite_runs_on_every_core_and_steals_queued_work` or
`test_the_mutation_driver_passes_the_distribution_on` every time (the
mutation table's W1 to W3). Without them it would read as an intermittent
failure rather than a red one.

**Closes when:** the suite holds a property only work stealing produces on
every run, for instance a holding worker given its whole half up front whose
tail is read back from xdist's own scheduling log, or the pins are accepted
as the guard and this is closed as such. Ledger only.

## R-1582. The mutation driver writes test ids verbatim, so a table whose rows kill the scan's own tests quotes their planted credentials and fails the scan

**Found:** at the mutation table of #686, U257, 2026-11-12.

**The mechanism.** `scripts/mutate.py` lists each killed test by its node id
in `docs/mutation/<unit>.md`. `tests/test_portability.py` parametrizes some

**Measured cost.** Maintainer facing only. The unit that measured it spells
the token as a placeholder in the table by hand and says so in it, which is
an edit to a measured record. No shipped verb pays for it.

**Closes when:** the driver elides a parametrized id's value where it would
write a credential shape (for example by running the table's text through
`secret_violations` and replacing each span with a placeholder before
writing), or the portability tests take ids that do not spell the body.

## R-1583. A mark appends without reading the applied ledger when its inode, size and modification time are as this process left them, whatever its bytes

**Found:** at the review of #683, U254, 2026-11-12.

**The mechanism.** `apply.mark_applied` skips the whole read of the journal
when `os.stat` gives the same device, inode, size and modification time this
process recorded after its own last write (`_LEFT`). An identity is not the
content: a file rewritten in place to the same size within one tick of the
volume's clock (a second on HFS+, two on FAT), or given its old time back with
`utime`, reads as untouched, and the record is appended to whatever it now
holds.

**Measured cost.** Driven by the review on a scratch copy: three marks, the
file overwritten in place with a schema 1 document of the same size and its
time restored, then one more mark. The record was appended to the document
and every later read refused it as not JSON, so `adopt apply` and the organize
reads stop until the file is repaired by hand. Four producers were driven and
none produces it: another process's mark (changes the size), a torn repair
across processes (a new inode), a staged restore of the same size and time (a
new inode, now pinned by a test), and an older build's mark (it refuses before
writing). An outside in place writer inside one clock tick is unmeasured.

**Closes when:** a mark proves the bytes it skips are the bytes it left, for
example a digest of the last record kept beside the identity and compared to
the file's tail, or the risk is accepted. Ledger only.

## R-1584. A journal's damaged last line is read as a torn mark and erased by the next mark, even when it was a whole record

**Found:** at the review of #683, U254, 2026-11-12.

**The mechanism.** `apply._read_journal` reads an unterminated last line, or
a terminated last line that is not JSON, as the mark that did not finish, and
the next mark rewrites the file without it. That is the decision U254's card
asked for and its tests hold: a mark is the last act of an entry, so a mark
that did not land re-applies the entry, which the module's crash table shows
is safe. Two inputs are dropped with it that were not torn writes: a whole
valid record that only lacks its newline, and a last line damaged by anything
other than a kill.

**Measured cost.** The documented re-apply: the entry is placed again (a no
op for the membership writer) and marked again, so its record's actor, digest
and applied time become the second run's. `organize/mirror_downloads.py` reads
the digest and the applied time to tell an adoption from a person's filing.
Neither producer (partial page persistence after a power loss, a hand edit)
was driven.

**Closes when:** the cost is accepted, or a whole record lacking only its
newline is kept and repaired with its newline rather than dropped. Ledger
only.

## R-1586. The adoption revert ledger is rewritten whole on every tag adoption writes, so its bytes grow with the square of the files tagged

**Found:** while building #683, U254, 2026-11-12.

**The mechanism.** `revert.write_tag_with_record` takes the adoption lock,
reads the whole revert ledger, adds or stamps one record, and writes the whole
ledger back through `config.atomic_write_bytes`, once per entry whose tag an
apply writes. U254 made the applied ledger a journal and left this one as it
was, because its records are changed in place after they are written (the
digest stamp, the list of entries that wrote through a record), so an append
needs update records and a fold of its own, not the applied ledger's.

**Measured cost.** Driven on this container with `write_tag_with_record` over
synthesized FLAC files in a scratch library: 50 records wrote 1,479,470 bytes,
100 wrote 5,873,770, 200 wrote 23,417,570, about 582 bytes a record and four
times the bytes for twice the records. By the fit, and not run, about 17.6 GB
for the 7,773 entries of the cutover's live apply, if every entry wrote a tag.
Only an apply that writes tags pays it; a membership only apply writes nothing
here.

**Closes when:** the revert ledger records each tag write without rewriting
itself whole, on a card of its own, with the same byte count test U254 uses.
Ledger only: a speed cost, none of the board bar's four.

## R-1591. A collection row whose ID is stored as bytes is left out of the check's scope of every row not analysed

**Found:** at the review of #688, U259, 2026-11-12.

**The mechanism.** When INBOX / Analyze holds no row, `crate analyze check`
reads every collection row rekordbox has not analysed. Before U259 it found
each one again by the text of its ID, and a row whose `djmdContent.ID` is
stored as bytes (`typeof(ID)` reads `blob`) was never found that way, so it
was silently left out of the scope. U259 judges each row in hand, which would
have put it in, and keeps it out on purpose (`_unanalysed` skips an ID that
is `bytes`; an ID stored as an integer, which the read by ID did find, is
kept, review round two), because the card required the check's answer to equal the
answer before it on every database. The skip is absence read as evidence of
absence, the first of METHOD's recurring patterns, kept rather than repaired.

**Measured cost.** Driven on a disposable copy with one row's ID cast to a
blob: the scope is the same rows before and after U259, and the cast row is
not named. No producer was driven: the column has text affinity, so it holds
bytes only if bytes are written to it, and neither rekordbox nor anything in
Cratekit was found writing them.

**Closes when:** someone decides whether such a row belongs in the scope
(named as unreadable, most likely, rather than silently absent) and the skip
in `_unanalysed` becomes that answer. Ledger only: no producer is known.

## R-1599. The brief's process wide parse memo was built, measured to cost more than it saved, and narrowed to one test

**Found:** at the build of #687, U258, 2026-11-12.

**The mechanism.** The brief asked that "within one test process a source text
is parsed once however many guards ask". The first build did exactly that,
`tests/_parse_memo.py` holding every tree for the life of the worker. Measured
on the touched test files in the suite's own distribution, four workers, two
runs each: wall time 85 to 91 s on `main` against 97 to 99 s with the memo, and
peak worker memory about 200 to 290 MB on `main` against 410 to 970 MB. Every
swept tree is about 720 MB in one process, and the collector walking that many
live nodes made the permissive soft delete sweep slower, 19.5 s to 22.5 s alone,
which outside pytest was 19.7 s without the memo, 25.0 s with it and 17.9 s with
it and the collector switched off. The guards that share files across tests
spend most of their time in analysis, so retaining trees bought them nothing.

The memo now shares a tree only inside one test that opens a scope, and drops
it when that test ends. The reading of the brief recorded here: its goal, that
a guard stops repeating the reading and parsing that feed it, is met inside
each test, where the repetition was; its literal wording, across tests, is not,
on measurement.

**Measured cost.** None to a person using the verbs. A future card that wants
cross test sharing would have to beat the collector's cost first; this entry
says what it is.

**Closes when:** ledger only. It records a decision and its measurement.

## R-1789. A behaviour capture cannot hold still a file's own times read back in the command that wrote it, or its change time, and no shipped scenario records one

**Found:** at the build of #854, U297, 2026-11-21.

**The mechanism.** The stage pins what Python reads: the clock, the two
duration clocks, the id sources and the rekordbox binding's row stamps. It
sets every file's access and modification time to the pinned instant between
commands (`Stage.settle`). It cannot set a change time at all, and a file
written and then read back for its times inside one command carries the real
time. A process id, a process start and a file's inode are read past by
`compare` where each capture holds its own. Product code that persists a
file's `st_mtime_ns` or `st_ctime_ns` taken in the same command (the library
index's cache key in `acquire/match.py`, the ISRC cache in
`acquire/isrc_cache.py`, the adoption fingerprint in `adopt/apply.py`) would
make two captures of one commit differ.

**Measured cost.** None today: the six shipped scenarios compare with no
difference, run after run. The first area card whose scenario reaches such a
write will see the equality test fail with the differing value shown, which
is loud, and will have to pair that value as a capture's own word or pin it.

**Closes when:** a scenario records such a value and the stage or `compare`
is taught it. Ledger only.

## R-1790. The suite's disposable rekordbox database is built with its foreign keys in an order that changes from one process to the next

**Found:** at the review of #854, U297, fix round one, 2026-11-21.

**The mechanism.** `tests/test_rb_writer_db.py::_relaxed_schema` copies the
binding's tables into a fresh `MetaData` with `Table.to_metadata`, which
walks each table's constraints as a set of objects. The copies take their
creation order from that walk, the order follows the objects' addresses, and
`create_all` writes the FOREIGN KEY clauses of each CREATE TABLE in it. A
fixed hash seed does not hold it still.

**Measured cost.** Found by the schema comparison the review asked for, on
its first run: two captures of one commit differed in five table
declarations. The review then built the schema in ten processes and read ten
distinct digests. `scripts/behaviour_compare.py` reads a table's declaration
with its table constraints in one order, which the review checked changes
nothing but that order over the 40 declarations of a real capture. No test
of the suite reads the declaration's text, so nothing else pays.

**Closes when:** the builder orders the copied constraints, at which point
`canonical_declaration` could go; the file is on U300's list. Ledger only.

## R-1791. killset.py list counts a row that broke collection and recorded a death as a recorded kill, 24 of 8,541, while check ranks it under one

**Found:** at the review of #854, U297, round one, 2026-11-21.

**The mechanism.** The reader calls a row killed when its table recorded at

one. Counted, never driven on a card.

the 20 are measured and those that break collection for a reason of the
row's own making are dropped from the set. Ledger only.

## R-1792. A new row can still pass as a replay by naming a recorded source and writing a moved reason

**Found:** at the review of #854, U297, round two, 2026-11-21.

**The mechanism.** A replay pass has no row cap because every row names a
recorded kill. `scripts/mutate.py` reads each source before anything runs:
the table exists under `docs/mutation/`, holds that row, the row recorded a
death, and a row whose file or text is not its source's must carry a `moved`
reason. A row anchored again by hand is exactly that, so the reason cannot be
refused, and nothing ties the new text to the old guard but the author's
word, which is committed in the table.

**Measured cost.** The review drove 26 new rows under real sources with no
reason, which is now refused. With a reason on each they would be measured.
Maintainer facing, and it takes an author writing 26 false reasons into a
committed table.

**Closes when:** it is accepted as the limit of a text based check, or
`killset.py check` is made the only reader of a replay table and holds a row
at new text to a test in common whoever wrote its reason. Ledger only.
